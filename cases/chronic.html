Your Path Forward: Understanding Your Chronic Issue

What's Going On & What to Expect

🟠
Chronic Case — Symptoms lasting 3 months or longer, a history of recurring episodes, or structural changes (degenerative disc disease, arthritis, long-standing postural problems). This doesn't mean your situation is hopeless — it means we approach it differently.

What's Happening in Your Body

Chronic spinal problems don't develop overnight, and they don't resolve overnight either. Over months and years, joints that weren't moving properly caused your spine to adapt — muscles shortened on one side, lengthened on the other, and your nervous system rewired itself around the dysfunction.

The result: your body has learned to live with a problem, and now we have to teach it something new. That process takes patience and consistency, but it works.

Many of our chronic patients tell us the same thing: they wish they'd started sooner. Not because it's a quick fix, but because consistent chiropractic care allows them to function better — less pain, more movement, fewer bad episodes — even when the underlying structural changes can't be fully reversed.

What we can do: Restore as much normal joint motion as possible, reduce the nervous system's pain signaling, slow the progression of degenerative changes, and help you feel better in your daily life.

What to be realistic about: Structural changes that developed over years won't disappear. But function and pain levels can improve significantly — and that's what changes your quality of life.

🩻
Spinal X-rays are recommended for chronic cases. Imaging lets us see the full extent of the changes that have taken place over time — where the spine has adapted, what's been affected structurally, and how to approach each phase of correction most effectively. We'll go over everything we find at your Day 2 appointment.

What to Expect Over the Coming Weeks and Months

Timeframe What You'll Likely Notice
Tonight & tomorrow Soreness is very common, especially on the first few visits. Your body is responding to movement it hasn't had in a long time. This is expected and not a cause for concern.
Visits 1–6 Progress may feel slow or non-linear. Some days will feel better, others similar to before. This is normal for chronic cases — the nervous system takes time to shift out of a long-held protective pattern.
Visits 7–12 Most patients begin to notice a clearer trend: pain episodes are less frequent, less intense, or shorter in duration. Movement improves. Daily activities feel more manageable.
Visit 11–12 Re-Exam We'll formally measure your progress — pain levels, range of motion, functional scores — and compare them to where you started. Then we build the next phase of your plan together.
Beyond the initial phase Continuing care is important to maintain and build on your progress. Chronic spinal patterns take time to fully unwind, and each phase of care corrects the spine further and reinforces what was gained before. We'll adjust our recommendations as you progress to keep things moving forward — and to make sure the ground you've gained doesn't slip back.

A Note on Flare-Ups

As your spine goes through the process of correction, your body may occasionally struggle against old patterns that have been in place for a long time. This can show up as a temporary flare-up — not because something went wrong, but because the body is working through deeply ingrained compensations.

Flare-ups aren't a part of the process, but they are possible. If a flare-up happens, it doesn't mean care isn't working — it means your body may temporarily need more support. We'll adjust your care as needed to provide that support, continue to build strength around the correction, and keep your healing moving forward. These adjustments to your plan based on what your body needs are a normal and intentional part of how we work.

If you notice a flare-up between visits, reach out to us right away so we can stay on top of your care and adjust your plan if necessary.

Your Job Between Visits

  • Manage soreness: Warm compress or shower on the area. Ice for acute flare-ups (15–20 minutes). Avoid prolonged heat on inflamed areas.
  • Stay as active as you can: Bed rest is almost never helpful for chronic spinal problems. Gentle movement — walking, light stretching — keeps the progress moving.
  • Sleep position: The most important focus is to get a good, restful night sleep. If needed, we'll give you specific guidance at Visit 2. In the meantime, avoid sleeping on your stomach if possible.
  • Notice your patterns: What makes it better or worse? Time of day, specific activities, stress levels? Bring that information to your next visit.
  • Notice what changes: Pay attention to any shifts in pain, stiffness, or movement after your adjustment — and roughly how long the benefit lasts before things plateau or start drifting back. For chronic cases, the window may be short at first — hours rather than days. That's expected, not discouraging. Each visit, note whether that window is getting longer. When it does, that's your nervous system starting to hold a new pattern. It's one of the clearest signs the work is accumulating.
  • Hydrate consistently. Chronically dehydrated discs are more vulnerable. This is a simple daily habit that supports everything we're doing.
